## s02_hypertension_diabetes: Hypertension and type 2 diabetes follow-up

**Subjective:** The patient reports headaches almost every evening for three weeks now. The headaches are at the back of the head and sometimes across the forehead; they feel heavy and pressing, not sharp. Paracetamol helps a little. The headaches worsen when stressed at work or skipping lunch. No vomiting, blurred vision, flashing lights, or weakness in arms/legs. No fever, neck stiffness, or recent fall/knock to the head. Patient was started on amlodipine 5mg once daily for blood pressure; patient misses it two or three times a week. Patient takes metformin 500mg twice daily with meals (morning dose only). Patient checks home blood pressure occasionally: one fifty over ninety and one forty-eight over ninety-two last week. Patient has a glucometer, morning sugar is around nine to ten, after meals it is twelve. Wakes up two times at night to pass urine; thirsty a lot. Weight loss of about 2 or 3 kg in the past two months. Tingling/pins and needles in feet (especially toes) for about two months, getting worse. No wounds/sores on feet that are slow to heal. No chest pain, palpitations, shortness of breath when climbing stairs. Mild ankle swelling by evening. Normal mood and sleep, but worried about health and work. Patient sleeps five hours a night.

**Objective:** Blood pressure today: 152/94 mmHg (repeat reading 148/92 mmHg). Pulse: 78 bpm, regular. Weight: 82 kg (3 kg less than last time). Temperature: Normal. Heart sounds: normal. Chest: clear. Abdomen: soft, no tenderness. Mild pitting edema at both ankles. Foot exam: intact skin, no ulcers. Pulses present at feet. Reduced sensation on big toes and front of both feet. Reduced ankle reflexes. Random blood sugar (finger prick): 11.2 mmol/L. Patient reports home blood pressure readings of 150/90 and 148/92 mmHg last week. Patient reports morning blood sugar around 9-10 before eating, and 12 after meals.

**Assessment (model):** The patient has uncontrolled hypertension (BP 152/94 mmHg) and hyperglycemia (random blood sugar 11.2 mmol/L). The patient also has peripheral neuropathy suggested by the tingling in the feet and reduced sensation on foot exam. Patient's lifestyle contributes to poor control of both conditions.

**Plan:** Increase amlodipine to 10 mg once daily, taken at the same time every morning. Increase metformin to 1 gram with breakfast and 500mg with evening meal, taken with food. Stop ibuprofen due to potential kidney strain and increased blood pressure. Patient should keep a diary of home BP and morning sugar for two weeks. Patient should bring the diary to next appointment. Eye check referral. ECG today. Follow-up in two weeks or sooner if headache is severe, vomiting, vision loss, chest pain, weakness on one side, or confusion. Advise patient to cut soda completely and reduce sugar in tea. Encourage walking at least 30 minutes five days a week. Limit beer to one on Saturdays. Patient's wife should accompany the patient for follow-up appointments. Patient should check feet daily for wounds/sores. If any are found, return immediately. Advise patient to wear closed shoes and inspect inside before putting them on. Encourage patient to reduce salt intake and choose grilled fish over fried. Provide written instructions regarding medication dosages and lifestyle modifications.

The patient reports that the son is a little warm at night, has no cough, and no rash. The patient reports that the son's sister had chickenpox last month, but he did not catch it. The patient reports that the son is not on any medicine and has no allergies that we know of. The patient reports that the son's vaccines are up to date.

The doctor advises the patient to keep breastfeeding and keep giving food. The doctor advises the patient to come back immediately if he sees blood in the stool, if he cannot drink, if he becomes very sleepy, or if the vomiting returns. The doctor advises the patient to bring the son back in two days.

## s05_asthma: Moderate asthma attack with an aspirin allergy

**Subjective:** The patient reports wheezing, coughing, and shortness of breath for two days. The patient states the patient is using salbutamol six to eight times a day. The patient denies fever and chest pain. The patient reports an allergy to aspirin. The patient does not smoke. The patient's brother smokes inside the house.

**Objective:** Breathing rate is twenty-four. Pulse is one hundred and four. Oxygen saturation is ninety-four percent. There is wheeze on both sides of the chest. Peak flow is two hundred and fifty. Best peak flow is about four hundred and fifty.

**Assessment (model):** Moderate asthma attack.

**Plan:** Give salbutamol through a spacer, four puffs now, and repeat. Give prednisolone forty milligrams once a day for five days. Start beclometasone two hundred micrograms twice a day, every day, even when the patient feels well. Ask the patient's brother to smoke outside. Return in two days for review. Return at once if the patient cannot finish sentences, if the patient's lips look blue, or if the blue inhaler stops helping.
